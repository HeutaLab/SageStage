This pass — the room. A teaching assistant, an SEN group or an EAL group is not standing three metres from a wall: they are at arm’s length with three or four children whose hands should be on it. The room setting in the gear carries the whole distance problem — type, targets, card width, density — and switching to a small group unlocks the board, because there the hands are the point. Earlier: a Year 6 cast — The cap moved off how many lines you can name and onto how many show at once. Three channels, because three separated colours is what a board carries — but name five if the story has five, park two heroes on the orange channel, and put one on air at a time. The one it displaces steps off, and nothing ever changes colour because something else was hidden. Earlier: writing room by age — The boxing-up page now rules itself for the class writing on it: EYFS block letters at 52px with a dashed midline to reach for, KS1 large, then narrower rules and more of them up the school — Years 5–6 gets nine lines to a box, because the story is longer. −/+ adds or removes room per box. The printed sheet uses the same pitch, so ruling no longer depends on how tall the Model column happened to be. Earlier: the flag is an event, not a sixth level — The scale still tops out at 5; ★ beyond is a separate mark for the word a class gives you once a term. Flagging one records a moment that carries its context — which box, which text, which stage, and who, if you say — because that is what a report can be written from. The gear shows what a moment becomes, next to the stock sentence it replaces. Moments print on the boxing-up sheet for the wall. Earlier: score is diction — A word’s score (1–5) says how rarely a primary writer reaches for it, not how strong the feeling is: wistful is mild and scores 5. Mood is a separate axis, and only mood decides counterpoint. And the moment worth being fastest at now has its own bar: type a word a child just offered, tap a score and a direction, Enter. It is in the bank before the moment passes. Earlier: a finer gauge — A word's reach is its direction and its climb together: sad reaches −1, desolate reaches −3. Measured against the story line (fixed at slot order, not whatever is armed), an attached word reads as serves it, one step short, one step stronger, or counterpoint. "One step short" is the flatness you spotted at the Problem, said out loud. Earlier: set a beat's tone and the words re-order to serve it — for this tone first, then neutral, then against it — on purpose. Nothing is hidden and nothing is called wrong: a happy beat written in gloom is counterpoint, which is what The Road is doing when its characters joke. Attach one and the beat says so, as a craft note. Earlier: a plan carries the mood each part usually runs at — a story mountain is an emotional shape before it is a list of boxes — so the gear can seed the target line from the plan itself and you argue with it from there. The bank checks itself against what this plan asks for rather than a neutral tally. Earlier: the bank groups by which part of the arc a word serves — for the lifts, the level, the falls — because the narrative pack is stocked almost entirely for the fall, and a class cannot write a +2 Opening out of gloom and trembling. Open a beat with a feeling set and the matching group is ringed. The bank says out loud when it is lopsided. Earlier: the graph now carries “we’re here → we want to be there.” A line is either where the writing is or where it should get to — target lines draw dashed with hollow markers, the gap between them is shaded, and every box states its own gap on the text map and on the boxing-up face. Closing that gap is what better word choice and sentence shape are for. Toggle a line between actual and target in the gear (Years 3–4 and up). Earlier: the pen now sits on every writing row, so it is beside the box you are in rather than a stretch back up the wall. Print offers sheet budgets — one A4 each for the children, or 8 sheets · about A1 for the wall, with the cut-and-tape lines shown. Face pills wear their own colour whether selected or not. Earlier: the workflow hole is closed — a new beat now opens with its text field focused, so you type what the class just said and press Enter to go straight into the next one. That is the loop that runs a hundred times a lesson. Pictures are picked from a row rather than cycled.
