# Sentence builder — a teacher's first walk-through

*For a colleague seeing it for the first time. No jargon, no research talk —
that lives in the design doc. Ten minutes with this page and you can run it.*

This widget is for **building and rehearsing one sentence out loud,
together** — combining two short sentences into a better one, growing a
plain one, or mending a broken one. It deliberately does *not* mark, score,
or count words, and the writing itself happens on paper, after the widget
has done its job. Think of it as the front half of a writing lesson.

## The traffic light

The three pills at the bottom are the lesson's traffic light. The colours
are always on show, so the class learns the format fast:

| Pill | Colour | Whose turn | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Model** | 🔴 red | *my turn* | Eyes on you. You build the sentence on the line, thinking aloud. |
| **Together** | 🟠 amber | *our turn* | Amber opens holding a **copy** of your modelled sentence. Press **↩ Deal it back** and the class watches the words peel off one by one and fly down into the tray. They rebuild it from memory, calling out; you place. |
| **Over to you** | 🟢 green | *your turn* | The screen goes quiet and shows the closing sentence plus the ritual: **1 Say yours out loud · 2 Write it · 3 Check with your talk partner.** Books open, widget done. |

Two rules the widget keeps for you:

- **Each stage keeps its own line.** Flip to red any time and your modelled
  sentence is sitting there, whole and untouched; flip back to amber and
  the class's half-built rebuild is exactly as they left it. Going back a
  step never costs the step.
- Amber only takes a fresh copy when you have modelled a **new** sentence —
  peeking never resets anything. The handover happens only when *you*
  press **Deal it back**.

## Say it — the three beats

Next to the pills: **beat · New · Practising · Fluent · 🗣 Say it**. The
three beats are a dial, not a play button: tap one to *set* the speed —
"are you ready for Fluent now?" is the cue, and the wait is part of it.
Only the teal **🗣 Say it** plays: the words light up one by one at the
chosen beat, then a sweep blends the whole sentence. There's no audio on
purpose — **you are the voice**; the widget keeps the beat.

The rhythm across a lesson: hear it **New** straight after you model it,
say it **Practising** while the class rebuilds, and **Fluent** — said like
talking — just before you hand over.

## A first lesson in eight taps (Combine)

1. **⚙ before the lesson**: pick *Combine*, then either type two short
   sentences (one per line — e.g. *Sara was hungry* / *She had not had
   breakfast*) or open **Sentence banks**, tap a topic, and **Use** a
   ready-made pair. Two taps and it's loaded.
2. On **red**, tap each sentence's **↓ deal** — its words drop into the
   tray.
3. Build your combined sentence on the line, thinking aloud. Add a joining
   word (*because*) from the yellow tiles and the full stop from the
   punctuation tiles.
4. Set the beat to **New** and press **🗣 Say it**. The class echoes each
   word.
5. Tap **Together** (amber), then **↩ Deal it back** — and let the class
   *watch* the words fly home to the tray. They rebuild the sentence by
   calling out while you place. Peek at red if anyone doubts a word —
   nothing is lost.
6. Beat to **Practising**, **🗣 Say it**, then tap **✓ Keep** — the
   sentence is saved to compare. If the class can build it a *different*
   good way, do that now and Keep that one too: two good answers side by
   side is the teaching.
7. Beat to **Fluent**, **🗣 Say it**, one last time, quick as talking.
8. Tap **Over to you** (green). The screen quietens to the sentence and the
   three steps: say yours, write it, check with your talk partner.

## The other faces, in one line each

- **Build** — a free line and a tray; the walkthrough above works the same,
  you just type your own words instead of dealing.
- **Expand** — your plain sentence starts on the line; the class grows it
  with *where? when? what like?* prompts.
- **Roles** — coloured question slots (*Who? Doing what? What? Where?*);
  cards live in the tray and the class drags them into slots; the sentence
  reads itself along the top. An empty slot shows ghost examples ("like…
  the dog · a girl") so nobody is stuck staring at nothing — swap in your
  own topic words in ⚙. Tap a waiting card once to pop its options.
- **Fix it** — has its own ceremony and its own buttons: the class reads
  the *correct* sentence first, you bring in the broken one, flag where the
  problem is, mend it by moving cards, and **always end on "Show it
  fixed."** The traffic light still shows whose turn it is, but Deal it
  back stays out of the way — mending, not rebuilding, is the point.

## Sentence banks — material for a term

⚙ holds **Sentence banks**: ready-made topics for your year (Pets, Romans,
Space…), each with Combine pairs, sentences to grow, and mends for Fix it.
Tap a topic, tap **Use** — it loads straight into the face you're
teaching. Below that, **Add your own — a term at a time**: paste a
term's worth in one go, one per line, with `" / "` between two sentences
to make a Combine pair. They file under *My import* for that year and
stay on this deck.

## Small things worth knowing

- **Pick cards up and they feel like magnets** — a ghost follows your
  finger, a teal bar shows where the card will land, and the slot or tray
  under it lights up. Drop somewhere that isn't a landing spot and the
  card just snaps home. Silly orders are a feature: build it wrong, Say
  it, let the class hear why.
- **Two dashed docks appear at the right of the tray while a card is in
  the air**: drop on **+1** for another copy (little words like "the" and
  "had" live many times in one sentence) — drop on **🗑** to bin it for
  good. No card in the air, no docks on screen.
- A word bank widget on the same screen feeds its words into the tray by
  itself — nothing to set up.
- Tap any card on the line for its options: flip the capital (aA — a dealt
  "They" can go lowercase mid-sentence), send it back to the tray (×), and
  in Fix it, the problem flag (⚑). Only the 🗑 dock deletes a card.
- **+ card** on the bar opens a little box for your own word or phrase —
  Enter adds it and keeps typing, so a list goes in fast.
- If the tray fills, the widget says so and keeps the overflow on the line
  rather than losing anything.
- The year group (⚙, or the deck's own) quietly decides which joining
  words, punctuation, slots — and which sentence-bank topics — are out.
  It is a filter, never a ladder on screen.
- Grammar names (*conjunction*, *subject*…) are an overlay you can switch
  on in ⚙ — the question words stay either way, and joining words on the
  line wear a little "joining word" tag while it's on.
- **Size the widget large and place it low** when children come to the
  board: the sentence lives in the upper half, everything they touch —
  tray, tiles, buttons — stays in reach in the lower half.
